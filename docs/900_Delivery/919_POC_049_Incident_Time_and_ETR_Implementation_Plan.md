# POC-049 — Incident time and ETR implementation plan

**Status:** Phases 0–4 implemented and committed. Phase 5 deterministic Copilot ETR assessment implemented as a candidate, pending owner review. Phase 6 remains planned.

**Scope:** Standard single-snapshot generator, multi-snapshot timeline generator, scenario/API contracts, incident detail, and deterministic AI Copilot ETR questions.

## Outcome

Give each newly generated incident a credible, immutable creation datetime within the requested date window. Let the operator choose the percentage of new incidents assigned to each initial ETR duration or duration range, including an intentional NULL ETR shown as **“Assessing Conditions.”** Make incident time, ETR state, and the scenario's simulated cutoff visible in the application. Answer questions such as “How many incidents have expired or coming due soon ETRs?” against the backend's current time, with five exhaustive categories: **Expired, Due Now, Due Soon, Valid Future, Unavailable**.

The scenario remains synthetic and frozen until the operator selects another snapshot. A changing ETR category does not mean that incident inventory, assignment state, or ETR values updated automatically.

## Example to review

Suppose a single scenario requests incident creation on September 28–29, 2026, with random local times. The operator sets the initial ETR mix to **25% at 8 hours, 25% at 24 hours, 25% at 48 hours, and 25% NULL**. Its explicit snapshot cutoff is **September 29 at 6:00 PM Eastern**. The operator asks Copilot at **September 29 at 8:00 PM Eastern**. These eight illustrative rows meet that mix exactly:

| Incident | Created (Eastern) | Stored ETR (Eastern) | At 8:00 PM |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Sep 29, 11:55 AM | Sep 29, 7:55 PM (+8h) | Due Now: 5 minutes past |
| B | Sep 29, 12:30 PM | Sep 29, 8:30 PM (+8h) | Due Soon |
| C | Sep 28, 10:00 AM | Sep 29, 10:00 AM (+24h) | Expired |
| D | Sep 29, 4:00 PM | Sep 30, 4:00 PM (+24h) | Valid Future |
| E | Sep 28, 9:00 AM | Sep 30, 9:00 AM (+48h) | Valid Future |
| F | Sep 29, 3:00 PM | Oct 1, 3:00 PM (+48h) | Valid Future |
| G | Sep 29, 1:00 PM | NULL: Assessing Conditions | Unavailable: assessing |
| H | Sep 28, 2:00 PM | NULL: Assessing Conditions | Unavailable: assessing |

Copilot would report **1 expired, 1 due now, 1 due within the next hour, 3 valid later, and 2 unavailable (both Assessing Conditions)**, evaluated at 8:00 PM using incident data frozen at 6:00 PM. An ETR of 7:50 PM would also be Due Now; 7:49 PM would be Expired. “Valid Future” describes the stored ETR's time category, not a guarantee that it remains operationally accurate. If the operator invokes this two-day scenario on September 28 at 6:00 PM, its September 29 snapshot is **ahead of real time**: it may be explored as an explicitly labeled simulation, but Copilot must not present these rows as a current-time operational count.

If asked **“How many ETRs have already passed?”**, Copilot should also give the derived past-due total: **2** in this example (the 1 Expired incident plus the 1 Due Now incident whose ETR was five minutes ago). This total is a view over the five exclusive categories, not a sixth category.

## Shared time contract

| Field | Meaning and rule |
| --- | --- |
| Incident `created_at` and `created_at_source` | When the synthetic incident record enters the scenario, plus its provenance. `created_at` is an aware RFC 3339 timestamp with offset, stable for that incident across timeline snapshots. It is **not** a separately known outage onset. New generated records use `created_at_source: synthetic_generator`; a later source-backed path may use `source_record`. Legacy missing values remain absent and display as unavailable. Never infer creation from the incident ID, `age_minutes`, ETR, file modification time, or manifest publication time. |
| Scenario `temporal.as_of_at` | Aware simulated cutoff for the complete inventory and state in that scenario folder. Every included incident must have `created_at <= as_of_at`. This is distinct from `freshness.updated_at`, which continues to describe generation/publication freshness. |
| Scenario `temporal.kind` | `single_snapshot` or `timeline_snapshot`. Timeline manifests also carry stable `timeline_id`, one-based `snapshot_index`, and `snapshot_count`; these identify sibling snapshots without relying on folder-name parsing. Snapshot `as_of_at` values must strictly increase within a timeline. |
| Incident `age_minutes` | Snapshot age for existing consumers. For newly generated records, derive it from `as_of_at - created_at` at each snapshot; do not increase it when a browser stays open. Existing scenarios retain their supplied values and the UI labels them **“Scenario snapshot age.”** Do not silently redefine ranking or filtering age as wall-clock age. |
| Incident `etr` and `etr_status` | `etr` is a stored aware instant or NULL. `etr_status` is `estimated` for a timestamp or `assessing_conditions` for an intentional NULL. Initial durations are selected from the configured distribution and measured from `created_at`. An intentionally NULL ETR remains NULL in later snapshots unless the timeline's **optional, configured assessing-to-ETR conversion** selects that incident at a step advance. Already estimated ETRs may still be revised by existing synthetic timeline rules. Existing records without a status retain their supplied ETR; a missing or unparsable legacy ETR is Unavailable with reason `missing_or_invalid`, not silently reclassified as intentional assessing. |
| Scenario `temporal.etr_distribution` and conversion policy | Retain the requested initial distribution and, for timelines, the assessing-to-ETR rule and any step overrides as generator provenance. The initial mix describes **new arrivals**; later inventories can differ as incidents arrive, clear, or gain an ETR through the configured conversion. |
| Copilot `evaluation_at` | Backend request time, captured once and returned with the answer. Calculate categories from this clock, independent of browser timezone or scenario publication time. Return the chosen scenario `as_of_at`, the hour-window size, category counts, evaluated incident count, and any temporal warning as typed response evidence. |

Keep the new CSV column and manifest object optional for existing scenario folders. Extend backend validation and API models additively; old fixtures still load. The frontend formats the stored offset in an explicit Eastern display and can reveal the original timestamp/source. In the incident detail, missing creation time reads “Created: unavailable”; no fallback should masquerade as a fact.

The generator needs a clear cutoff input, proposed as `--as-of-at` in Eastern time (with explicit offset in output). For a single snapshot, `--start-date`/`--end-date` are inclusive **candidate creation dates**, but sampled creation times must be no later than `as_of_at`. Reject an empty or inconsistent eligible window rather than producing future incidents. Dry-run output should state the requested dates, cutoff, actual earliest/latest creation times, requested ETR mix, and realized counts before any write. The existing preview-first and no-overwrite behavior remains.

## Configurable initial ETR mix

Use one shared parameter contract in the standard CLI and the timeline CLI or its interactive prompts, proposed as `--etr-mix '8h=25,24h=25,48h=25,null=25'`. A fixed duration such as `8h` is a one-point range; a band such as `4-8h=40` samples an elapsed duration within that band. The values are percentages of **new incidents at creation**, not percentages of ETRs remaining at query time. Without an explicit mix, retain the previously proposed default of 100% sampled from 4–24 hours and 0% NULL; “Assessing Conditions” is the display default **when an intentional NULL is generated**, not a default share of incidents.

Validate that shares are nonnegative, total 100%, durations/ranges are positive and ordered, and NULL has at most one entry. Apply a deterministic allocation with rounding so realized counts sum to the cohort size; for small cohorts, exact percentages may be impossible, so show both target and realized counts. Randomize which incidents receive each allocation with a seed stream independent of geography and creation-time draws. For a duration band, sample an elapsed minute within its inclusive bounds; fixed durations remain exact. A generated NULL has blank `etr` plus `etr_status: assessing_conditions`, never a fake future timestamp. The same mix applies to the initial cohort and **each later arrival cohort** in a timeline. An incident's initial NULL allocation remains NULL while it is present unless the optional timeline conversion selects it at a later step; clearance and later arrivals can also change the active inventory's proportions.

In all incident ETR displays, use one formatter: an estimated timestamp is shown with timezone; an intentional NULL reads **“Assessing Conditions”** in place of the timestamp. A legacy blank or malformed ETR with no explicit assessing status reads **“ETR unavailable.”** The five-bucket Copilot result counts both cases under Unavailable, with a breakdown of `assessing_conditions` versus `missing_or_invalid` so operators can tell a modeled assessment from a data defect.

### Optional timeline conversion of Assessing Conditions

The timeline generator already exposes `--snapshots` (1–10) and positive `--hours-apart`; **5 snapshots and 12 hours apart are defaults, not fixed timing rules**. Apply the conversion rule to the actual configured transitions, from step 2 through step N, and evaluate ETRs against each step's aware `as_of_at` value.

**Recommended parameter shape:** one rule for every transition from step 2 through step N, with optional overrides keyed by destination step. For example, `--assessing-conversion '10%@24h'` means that at each advance, select about 10% of the **continuing active incidents still marked Assessing Conditions** and give each an ETR equal to that destination snapshot's `as_of_at + 24 elapsed hours`. An override such as `--assessing-conversion-overrides '2=10%@12h,4=20%@24h'` changes only steps 2 and 4. Step numbering is one-based; step 1 has no conversion because no advance has occurred. The same baseline rule works for any supported snapshot count, avoiding a required parameter for every step. The interactive generator should ask for the baseline once and request overrides only if wanted.

Default conversion is **off**: omitting the rule leaves intentional NULLs as Assessing Conditions in all later snapshots, preserving the simpler behavior. If enabled, validate the percentage from 0–100, a positive horizon, unique override step numbers in `2..snapshots`, and that a one-snapshot timeline has no conversion. An override replaces the baseline for its step; `0%@12h` explicitly disables that step. Use deterministic seeded selection and a rounding policy that carries fractional remainders between steps, so small pools do not systematically round to zero or one on every advance.

At each advance, clear incidents first, then apply conversion to the still-active NULL incidents **carried from the prior snapshot**, then add new arrivals with the configured initial ETR mix. New arrivals must keep their initial status in their first snapshot. A converted record changes from `assessing_conditions`/NULL to `estimated`/a timestamp exactly once; retain a generated `etr_updated_at` equal to the destination snapshot cutoff and `etr_source: timeline_assessment_conversion` for traceability. Selection is a synthetic scenario event, not a crew recommendation, assignment action, or user-entered ETR. Already estimated ETRs are outside the conversion pool.

With 100 continuing NULL incidents and no arrivals or clearances, a 10% repeat rule yields approximately 10 conversions at step 2, 9 at step 3, 8 at step 4, and 7 at step 5. The actual active NULL count can rise when new NULL incidents arrive. The conversion horizon must be compared with the **actual elapsed interval to the next snapshot**: a shorter horizon is already past at the next step, an equal horizon is Due Now, and a longer horizon is still Valid Future. Thus `+12h` is Due Now at the next step under the **default** 12-hour interval; `+24h` remains future then. If the operator changes `--hours-apart` to 24, `+24h` becomes Due Now at the next step and a longer horizon is needed to remain future. Compare aware UTC instants across daylight-saving changes rather than assuming wall-clock labels are always the configured number of elapsed hours apart. Future-ETR totals still cannot be guaranteed to increase monotonically because earlier ETRs age and incidents clear. Preview each step's active Assessing Conditions, conversions, future ETRs, due/expired ETRs, and arrivals so the generated story can be reviewed before publication.

If a five-step, 12-hour-apart story needs a stronger rise in future ETR coverage, keep the baseline rule and use step overrides to increase the conversion rate, such as **10%, 20%, 30%, 40% at steps 2–5**, each with a `+24h` horizon. In a simplified 100-NULL cohort with no arrivals or clearances, that yields about **10, 28, 40, 42 future ETRs** at those steps; the preview is authoritative when real arrivals, clearance, and rounding are included. These overrides are optional narrative controls, not four mandatory parameters for every timeline. For a different snapshot count or interval, build the override list and horizon from that configuration.

## Clock policy for Copilot

“Current” means the backend request time. For an eligible snapshot, classify every active incident in its complete inventory, including records withheld from recommendation ranking. Do not count only visible table pages, selected records, or the Copilot provider's bounded context.

| Selected scenario | Current-time ETR answer |
| --- | --- |
| Single snapshot with `as_of_at <= evaluation_at` | Allowed. Show **“ETRs evaluated at [now]; incident data as of [cutoff]”** and disclose frozen inventory/ETRs. |
| Timeline snapshot with `as_of_at <= evaluation_at` and no later sibling already due | Allowed with the same two-clock disclosure. |
| Snapshot with `as_of_at > evaluation_at` | Block a current-time count. Explain how far ahead the simulated state is; offer a separately labeled **scenario preview as of its simulated time**, or selection of an eligible earlier snapshot. Do not mix future inventory with today's clock. |
| Timeline snapshot with a later sibling whose `as_of_at <= evaluation_at` | Do not claim a complete current assessment from the older snapshot. Prompt selection of the latest eligible sibling; an explicitly labeled historical preview may remain available. |
| Legacy scenario without authoritative `as_of_at` | Show incident creation if supplied, but do not claim current-time scenario completeness. Explain that temporal status cannot be validated; allow a clearly labeled ETR-only preview if added, with no operational “current” claim. |

The backend should discover sibling manifests through trusted scenario metadata, validate timeline identity and ordering, and compute the latest eligible snapshot at request time. A user-selected snapshot is never auto-advanced. If timeline metadata is missing, duplicated, or inconsistent, fail closed on the current-time claim and give a specific reason. A later snapshot scheduled after now does not make the currently selected eligible snapshot stale.

A **scenario preview** uses the selected snapshot's `as_of_at` as the simulated evaluation clock and says so in the response; it is never labeled “current.” A historical preview of an older timeline snapshot uses that older cutoff. This explicit mode is separate from the default current-time question, so a blocked current-time request cannot silently become a simulated answer.

For reproducible boundaries, compare UTC instants after normalizing ETR and `evaluation_at` to minute precision. **Expired:** ETR minute more than 10 minutes before the evaluation minute. **Due Now:** ETR minute from 10 minutes before through the evaluation minute, inclusive; this includes recently passed ETRs. **Due Soon:** later than the evaluation minute and no more than 60 minutes ahead. **Valid Future:** more than 60 minutes ahead. **Unavailable:** intentional Assessing Conditions or missing/invalid ETR, with those reasons broken out. These buckets are mutually exclusive and their counts sum to the complete evaluated incident count. Show both the 10-minute grace window and the 60-minute forward window in the answer.

For wording such as “expired already” or “past due,” also return `past_due_total`: every timed ETR minute before the evaluation minute, comprising Expired plus the past portion of Due Now. Expose that breakdown so the ten-minute Due Now convention cannot hide a recently passed ETR. An ETR in the current minute is Due Now but not yet included in the minute-precision past-due total.

Use an **absolute one-hour Due Soon window** for this POC. It has the same operational meaning for an 8-hour and a 48-hour ETR: action is needed within the next hour. A percentage of original duration would turn the same alert into different remaining hours across incidents (for example, 12.5% means 1 hour for an 8-hour ETR but 6 hours for a 48-hour ETR), making the aggregate harder to act on. A future separate “fraction of ETR remaining” early-warning metric can be added without changing these five categories. The backend should expose the forward-window value as response evidence and use one centrally configured policy value if it later becomes adjustable; it is not a per-scenario generation parameter.

## Generator design

### Standard single snapshot

1. Select seeded random creation instants across eligible minutes of the entered local date range through `as_of_at`, distributing incidents across eligible dates as requested rather than clustering them all on one day. Use timezone-aware instants so DST gaps/overlaps cannot create nonexistent or ambiguous local times; serialize offsets. Preserve deterministic output for a seed and parameter set. Derive the date-bearing incident ID from the selected local creation date.
2. Allocate the configured initial ETR mix to new incidents. For non-NULL allocations, choose the fixed or in-band duration after `created_at`; for NULL, write an explicit assessing status. Keep existing generator constraints for incident geography, customer totals, critical facilities, and topology. Isolate time and ETR random draws from existing allocation random streams where practical, then measure any unavoidable changes to seeded fixtures and scoring.
3. Reconcile assignment creation/progression with `created_at`: no assignment event can precede incident creation, and no generated event or state can be later than `as_of_at`. Derive snapshot `age_minutes` from creation time rather than choosing an unrelated 5–180 minute value.
4. Write `created_at` and provenance to `PendingIncidents.csv` and typed temporal metadata to `scenario.json`; validate the whole folder with `ScenarioService` before publication.

### Multi-snapshot timeline

1. Define the requested start/end dates as the **overall arrival window** for the sequence, while `start_time` and `hours_apart` define snapshot cutoffs. Validate that the schedule intersects the requested arrival window; do not seed the first snapshot with incidents from later dates or later hours. The current first-snapshot call to the standard generator over the full date range must be changed accordingly.
2. At snapshot 1, create only incidents with `created_at <= first as_of_at` and apply the configured ETR mix to that cohort. For each subsequent snapshot, sample new incident creation instants in `(previous_as_of_at, current_as_of_at]`, further restricted by the overall arrival window, and apply the same mix to the new-arrival cohort. Preserve each continuing incident's `created_at`; recalculate its snapshot age. Cleared incidents leave the active inventory according to existing timeline behavior.
3. Preserve `etr_status: assessing_conditions` and NULL `etr` for continuing assessing incidents unless the configured step conversion selects them. The current timeline code automatically writes an ETR for both assigned and unassigned incidents at each snapshot; guard that rewrite so only an enabled conversion can move an intentional NULL to an estimate. Generated crew assignment alone must not do so. Existing synthetic revisions to already estimated ETRs can continue. Validate the NULL/status pairing, conversion provenance, and continuity across siblings.
4. Store the typed timeline identity, index/count, cutoff, initial ETR mix, and conversion policy in every sibling manifest. Keep the current staging, deterministic-seed, preflight, and all-or-nothing publication protections. Preview summaries should show arrivals, clearances, creation-time range, requested/realized ETR mix per arrival cohort, eligible NULL pool, converted count, remaining Assessing Conditions, future ETR count, estimated ETR revisions, and chronology violations for every snapshot.

## Application and Copilot design

- Making or accepting a crew recommendation in the running application does **not** request an ETR from the operator and does **not** manufacture or change one automatically. The optional timeline conversion happens only while generating later scenario snapshots. The application reads the ETR state supplied by the selected snapshot; a future incident-ETR editing workflow would need its own contract and approval.
- Extend scenario and incident API types before changing presentation, including nullable `etr` and its explicit status. Surface the selected scenario cutoff near its freshness label, clearly distinguishing **“Scenario data as of”** from **“Scenario last updated.”** In incident detail show `created_at`, timezone, provenance, snapshot age, and the ETR or “Assessing Conditions.” Apply the shared ETR formatter wherever incidents appear. Keep legacy unavailable fields honest.
- Add a prominent temporal-state message when the selected snapshot is ahead of now or behind the latest eligible timeline snapshot. The message should name the relevant time and guide the operator to an eligible snapshot; it must survive scenario switching and work at narrow widths.
- Route explicit ETR time/count questions to a deterministic backend handler before bounded provider synthesis. Use one captured request clock and the complete active incident inventory. Return all five buckets, the evaluated total, scenario cutoff, evaluation time, window, and a few traceable incident examples. Never ask the model to infer aggregate counts from five context records.
- Reuse the same temporal policy for any UI summary or follow-up question so the app cannot show a current count that Copilot blocks. Keep existing Copilot `data_snapshot_at` semantics for other intents; add typed ETR-specific fields rather than silently repurposing a weather snapshot timestamp.

## Phased implementation and review gates

### Phases 0–2 candidate (2026-10-26)

The initial implementation requires an explicit offset-bearing `--as-of-at` to opt into the new single-snapshot contract. Without it, the standard generator and existing timeline generator retain their legacy output until Phase 3 updates timeline semantics. In temporal mode, the default ETR mix is `4-24h=100`; `--etr-mix` can specify fixed hours, hour bands, and intentional NULL shares. The new CSV fields and manifest `temporal` object are optional for legacy folders, and generated temporal folders pass `ScenarioService` preflight before write completion. The application has minimal NULL-safe ETR rendering and sorting, labels incident age as scenario snapshot age, and gives an honest selected-incident Copilot ETR label; the fuller incident-time presentation and time-based Copilot answers remain Phases 4 and 5.

An isolated 24-incident preview using September 28–29 dates, a September 29 6 PM Eastern cutoff, and `8h=25,24h=25,48h=25,null=25` produced six incidents in each bucket. Its 32 crews, seven assignments, creation times, assignment chronology, and scenario/API round trip validated. This preview is outside `data/scenarios/` and is not a published fixture. It is a contract and generator sample, not yet a complete five-category Copilot demo: at its simulated cutoff it has expired, future, and assessing incidents, but no Due Now or Due Soon incident. Phase 5 needs a targeted boundary fixture for those categories.

A separate 2,000-incident, 500-crew preview with the same dates, cutoff, and mix also passed isolated folder preflight. It produced 500 incidents in each ETR category and 105 assignments. Both previews remain under `/private/tmp/poc049-phase02-current/` for local review.

### Phase 3 candidate (2026-10-26)

The timeline CLI now uses the temporal contract by default with `4-24h=100` unless `--etr-mix` supplies another distribution. The Python `generate_timeline` entrypoint retains its legacy default when called without temporal arguments so existing callers and scenarios remain compatible. `--start-date` and `--end-date` bound incident arrivals across the sequence; when the end date is omitted, the CLI defaults it to the configured final snapshot's local date. Each snapshot advances by configured elapsed hours, including across daylight-saving transitions. The first snapshot contains only incidents created by its cutoff. Later arrivals receive a seeded creation minute and the initial ETR mix for their own cohort. Continuing incidents retain `created_at`, and their age is recalculated at each cutoff.

Optional `--assessing-conversion '10%@24h'` applies to continuing active Assessing Conditions incidents after clearances and before arrivals at each step. `--assessing-conversion-overrides '2=10%@12h,4=20%@24h'` replaces the baseline at named destination steps. Conversion is off by default; its seeded selection carries fractional rounding forward, and each selected incident records `etr_updated_at` and `etr_source: timeline_assessment_conversion`. Assignment alone leaves an intentional NULL ETR unchanged. Snapshot manifests retain timeline identity and the configured policy. The CLI dry run reports per-step arrivals, clearances, eligible and converted counts, ETR categories with examples, eligible/future/stale clock state, and a warning when a conversion horizon reaches or precedes the next cutoff.

Isolated 1-, 5-, and 10-snapshot previews with varied intervals passed application preflight under `/private/tmp/poc049-phase3-preview/`. The 5-step sample with 80 initial incidents, 12-hour intervals, a 25%/25%/50% 8-hour/24-hour/NULL mix, and conversion overrides had 80, 75, 54, 31, and 20 active incidents. A separate 2,000-incident, 500-crew five-step preview also passed preflight. Its active Assessing Conditions counts were 500, 428, 296, 145, and 84, while valid future and due-soon ETRs together were 1,500, 1,017, 441, 287, and 143 as incidents aged and cleared. These are generator previews, not published fixtures.

### Phase 4 candidate (2026-10-26)

The application now separates the selected scenario's Eastern **data-as-of cutoff** from its existing publication freshness time. It labels frozen single snapshots, latest eligible timeline snapshots, future snapshots, older selected timeline snapshots, and timelines whose sibling metadata cannot be verified. Future and older selections show a prominent notice; where an eligible sibling exists, the notice offers a direct selection action. The selected snapshot never advances automatically. Incident detail shows Eastern creation time and provenance, frozen scenario snapshot age, Eastern ETR, and timeline conversion provenance when present. All incident ETR surfaces share the distinction between intentional NULL **Assessing Conditions** and missing or malformed legacy **ETR unavailable**. Legacy creation time remains unavailable rather than being inferred.

An isolated browser preview covered a Phase 2 single snapshot, the Phase 3 five-step timeline, a future single snapshot, and the checked-in legacy base scenario. It checked future and older notices, selecting the latest eligible sibling, intentional NULL and converted ETR details, legacy missing creation time, scenario switching, Eastern timezone labels, desktop layout, and a 390-pixel phone layout. These previews were loaded from temporary paths; no scenario fixture was published. Phase 6 remains the owner release and fixture-publication decision.

### Phase 5 candidate (2026-10-26)

Explicit ETR time/count questions now use a deterministic backend query before provider synthesis. One captured backend request time classifies the complete active incident inventory, including ranking-withheld records, into Expired, Due Now, Due Soon, Valid Future, and Unavailable. Typed response evidence includes the selected cutoff, evaluation time, 10-minute grace and 60-minute forward windows, total, five counts, past-due total and its Due Now portion, unavailable reason split, and traceable incident examples. The existing weather `data_snapshot_at` field is not presented as an ETR cutoff.

Current-time counts are blocked for future snapshots, older timeline selections with a later eligible sibling, timelines with incomplete or inconsistent sibling metadata, and legacy scenarios without an authoritative cutoff. The separate **Scenario preview at selected cutoff** control allows an explicitly labeled simulated answer for a valid future or historical temporal snapshot; it does not auto-advance the selected scenario. Legacy and unverifiable timelines remain blocked in preview mode. The current-time path uses the backend clock, while the UI continues to show selected-snapshot guidance from Phase 4.

Boundary tests cover exactly 10 minutes past, current minute, exactly 60 minutes ahead, missing/invalid and intentional NULL ETRs, withheld incidents, future/stale/legacy gating, explicit preview, and routing before provider synthesis. An isolated end-to-end service check against the Phase 2 single preview and Phase 3 five-step preview produced complete counts: at September 29, 2026 9:00 PM Eastern, the single snapshot had 24 incidents (7 Expired, 0 Due Now, 1 Due Soon, 10 Valid Future, 6 Unavailable); timeline step 4 had 31 (16, 1, 0, 5, 9). Step 1 was blocked as stale, step 5 as future, and step 5 preview returned a separately labeled 20-incident result. Browser review confirmed current and preview answers and a blocked future single snapshot. No preview scenario was published.

### Scenario-quality review before publication

Passing schema and chronology checks is necessary but does not establish that a generated scenario is useful for time-based Copilot questions. For each selected snapshot cutoff, the preview should show active incidents, arrivals and clearances, Assessing Conditions, newly converted ETRs, Expired, Due Now, Due Soon, and Valid Future counts, plus a few traceable example incidents. Compare the chosen conversion horizon with the **configured** interval between snapshots and warn when converted ETRs will already be due or expired at the next step. Check that the requested mix and conversion rules produce the intended narrative: a plausible shift from assessing to estimated incidents and enough future, near-due, and past-due examples at the steps where they are meant to be queried. A monotonic future-ETR count is not required because ETRs age and incidents clear. If the preview does not support the intended questions, adjust the parameters and regenerate the isolated preview before publishing; do not silently alter percentages or ETRs to force an attractive chart.

Targeted tests should cover dates spanning midnight, fixed 8/24/48-hour and bounded-range ETRs, distribution totals and rounding for small cohorts, 100% NULL and 0% NULL, default-off conversion, repeat conversion and step overrides, small-pool rounding, no same-step conversion of new arrivals, no repeat conversion of estimated incidents, and no ETR change from recommendation actions in the application. With 12-hour steps, verify that a converted `+12h` ETR reaches Due Now at the next step while `+24h` remains future; with 24-hour steps, `+24h` reaches Due Now and `+48h` remains future. Check step-override validity for 1, 5, and 10 snapshots and elapsed-time comparisons across DST.
